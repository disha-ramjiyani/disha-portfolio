Executive Summary

KoRo is a Berlin-based D2C food company built on five principles: quality, short supply chains, bulk packaging, fair prices, and transparency. It's grown from a German e-commerce startup into a pan-European brand stocked in 10,000+ retail stores, holds B Corp and Bio-Siegel organic certification, and recently closed a €35M Series C round.

India is projected to become the world's 3rd-largest economy by 2027, with a processed food market expected to grow from $535B (2026) to $1 trillion by 2035. Within that, the healthy snacks segment (nuts, seeds, and dried fruit especially) is the fastest-growing category and directly maps to KoRo's existing product line. With 100% FDI allowed in food processing and roughly 40% of India's population vegetarian, the conditions line up well for a brand that's already 98% vegetarian and 92% vegan.

Estimated Market Size

India's processed food market splits into five categories. KoRo's product line spans three of them: Healthy Snacks, Functional Foods, and Organic & Natural Foods, together representing roughly 70% of the addressable category.

Target Market

We sized the opportunity by layering age, income, and lifestyle segments: youth (18–35) and middle-age (35–55) consumers make up roughly 65% of the population and skew fitness-focused and health-conscious, while the middle-to-upper income brackets (about 20% of the population) are the realistic near-term buyers for a premium import. Even at 20% of India's 1.4 billion people, that's an addressable market of ~280 million, more than twice the population of Germany.

SWOT Analysis

Weighing KoRo's own position against the Indian market it'd be entering:

Strengths

  • B Corp + Bio-Siegel organic certification build instant credibility
  • 98% vegetarian / 92% vegan product line matches Indian dietary needs
  • Proven bulk-packaging model keeps prices competitive at scale
  • €35M Series C funding to back international expansion

Weaknesses

  • No prior expansion outside Europe, so no existing playbook for this market
  • European flavor profiles need localization for Indian palates
  • No existing brand awareness or trust with Indian consumers

Opportunities

  • India's healthy snacks market growing at 8.1% CAGR to $8.18B by 2033
  • 100% FDI allowed in food processing, plus multi-year tax exemptions
  • Relatively few premium healthy-bar options in the market today
  • 280M-person addressable segment of health-conscious consumers

Threats

  • Landed cost runs 150–180% of ex-factory price after duties, GST, and freight
  • Established local competitors (Yoga Bar, MuscleBlaze, Happilo) already have distribution
  • India's high power-distance, collectivist culture requires a different trust-building approach than Europe

Competitive Landscape

India's healthy snack space is already active: Yoga Bar, The Whole Truth, MuscleBlaze, Happilo, Bagrry's, and newer entrants like SuperYou all compete on protein bars, trail mixes, and clean-label snacking. KoRo's differentiation is its European-import credibility, bulk-value pricing model, and broader range spanning nut butters, freeze-dried fruit, and organic staples. Most competitors are narrower, either sports-nutrition-first or gifting-first.

Cultural Analysis (Hofstede)

Comparing Germany and India on Hofstede's cultural dimensions surfaced the clearest gaps in power distance and individualism: India is more hierarchical and more collectivist, which reshapes how trust gets built. Marketing needs to lean on credible experts and testimonials rather than direct-to-consumer bravado, and messaging should center family and sharing rather than individual achievement.

Marketing Mix Recommendation

Product: Lead with nut butters, trail mixes, freeze-dried fruit, protein bars, and organic oats: high cultural familiarity and low regulatory friction. Localize flavor (masala peanut butter, mango-chilli protein bars) rather than importing the German lineup unchanged.

Price: A tiered value strategy positioned above mass-market local brands but below hyper-premium imports, using small trial SKUs (100g sachets) to lower the barrier to a first purchase, plus a subscription discount to build repeat behavior.

Place: An e-commerce-first phased rollout: Amazon India, Flipkart, and a localized KoRo.in D2C site in Phase 1; quick-commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) and modern trade partnerships once brand equity is established.

Promotion: Digital-first and influencer-led, anchored in fitness and clean-eating content on Instagram/YouTube, micro-influencer partnerships, and festive gifting campaigns (Diwali, Holi, Raksha Bandhan) that lean into India's collectivist, gifting-oriented culture.
